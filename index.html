How the peg pyramid works

The board forms a triangle of staggered pegs, with each row one peg wider than the one above it. A ball dropped at the top has an even chance of moving left or right, and that same split repeats at every row. After all rows are crossed, it lands in one of the bottom pockets, which are always one more than the number of rows.

Because each path is built from fifty-fifty turns, balls cluster around the middle and only rarely travel to the far edges. Statisticians call this a Galton board, and the shape it creates is a binomial distribution. The payout table mirrors that curve: central pockets pay less, while rare edge pockets pay more.

Multipliers, odds, and the house margin

Each pocket value is set close to the inverse of its probability, then trimmed by a small house margin. If you multiply each payout by the chance of landing there, the total comes out a little below one, and that gap is the operator's edge. In many versions of the game, the return to player sits around 97% to 99%, depending on the studio and the chosen settings.

A useful reality check: no row count or risk level turns the long-term result in your favour. The settings only change variance, meaning how bumpy the session feels, not the average outcome.

Trusted releases use a certified random number generator for every deflection, while provably fair editions publish seed hashes so a finished round can be reviewed independently. No version gives you a way to steer the ball.

Straight answers to five questions

Is every drop genuinely random?

On licensed platforms, yes. Each deflection comes from an audited random number generator, and provably fair versions let you confirm later that the path was fixed before the ball moved.

Do more rows make the game pay better?

No. Extra rows add pockets and stretch the odds curve, so edge prizes grow while their probability falls. The overall return stays about the same across board sizes.

Which risk level is the smart choice?

None is mathematically better. Low risk smooths the ride, while high risk concentrates value in rare edge hits. Choose based on the amount of swing your budget can handle.

Can I practise without spending anything?

Yes. The board on this page runs on virtual credits, and most operators keep a free mode with the same odds. The maths does not change when real money is added.

Does timing or button rhythm affect where a ball lands?

No. The path is set by the random generator, not by when or how fast you press drop. Any pattern you notice is coincidence, not control.
